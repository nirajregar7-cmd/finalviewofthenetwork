Redesign the homepage of The Network around our actual vision and founder story.

The Network is NOT only a college networking platform. It is a student network for school students, college students, competitive exam aspirants and students exploring different skills and interests across India.

The core positioning should be:

“Your Network Should Be Bigger Than Your Surroundings.”

The Network helps students connect beyond the boundaries of their own school, college, city or surroundings and discover people across India who share their interests, goals and ambitions.

The homepage should feel like a real student-built startup with a strong mission. It should be clean, modern, youthful and trustworthy. Avoid making it look like a generic social media app or a corporate EdTech website.

IMPORTANT:
- Do NOT add a Founding Member section.
- Do NOT make the homepage college-specific.
- Do NOT position The Network as only a college platform.
- Do NOT overuse corporate language.
- Keep the existing brand identity and UI style where possible, but restructure the homepage around the vision below.
- The homepage should clearly communicate that students can connect with people from their own school/college/city/village AND with students across India.
- Do not claim features that are not currently available. Clearly distinguish the current product from the long-term vision.

8. WHY WE STARTED THE NETWORK
--------------------------------------------------

Heading:

I Didn’t Know What Was Possible.

This section should be visually strong and personal.

Founder story:

I come from a small village near Bidasar, Churu, Rajasthan.

I studied Classes 11 and 12 in a government school in my village.

At that time, I didn’t know much about coding, robotics, aerospace, startups or the different skills and career paths students could explore.

I didn’t really know what I could learn or what I could actually do beyond what I was seeing around me.

There weren’t many people around me who could introduce me to these things or guide me towards them.

After Class 11, a friend told me about JEE.

At that time, I couldn’t join a coaching institute or move to another city.

After Class 12, when I got good marks, someone suggested to my father that he should send me to Sikar for further preparation.

That’s when things started changing for me.

Later, I got into NIT Trichy.

But when I first came here, I struggled a lot.

I wasn’t even comfortable with English in the beginning, and my first semester was difficult.

Then I started seeing something I had never really experienced before.

Students around me had already been learning coding since Class 11 or 12.

Some were building projects.

Some already knew about different technologies, competitions, startups, research opportunities and different career paths.

And I kept thinking:

“Why didn’t I know about these things earlier?”

It wasn’t that I didn’t want to learn.

I simply didn’t have the exposure.

I didn’t have people around me who could show me what was possible.

That experience made me realise:

“A student’s potential shouldn’t be limited by their surroundings.”

That’s why we’re building The Network.

DESIGN DIRECTION
--------------------------------------------------

Use a clean, modern, student-focused visual identity.

The homepage should feel:
- youthful
- ambitious
- human
- trustworthy
- inclusive
- India-focused
- student-built

Avoid:
- generic corporate stock imagery
- overly formal EdTech styling
- excessive gradients
- unnecessary animations
- fake testimonials
- fake statistics
- “corporate” buzzwords
- making everything about colleges
- making the product look like LinkedIn

Use visual storytelling where possible.

The founder story should feel personal and authentic.

The central visual idea throughout the homepage should be:

“Your Network Can Be Much Bigger Than Your Surroundings.”

The website should make it immediately clear that The Network is for students from villages, towns, cities, schools, colleges and different educational backgrounds — and that they can connect both with people around them and with students across India.

The final emotional takeaway should be:

“I don’t have to be limited by the people and opportunities around me. I can build a much bigger network.”
